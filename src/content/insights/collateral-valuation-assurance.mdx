## Executive insight

Most collateral risks do not originate at sanction—they emerge silently post-disbursement.

## The hidden risk

During a recent credit review of a mid-sized Loan Against Property (LAP) portfolio, a bank identified multiple exposures backed by collateral with expired insurance, outdated legal reports, and incomplete documentation. These vulnerabilities did not arise from negligence at the sanction stage; rather, they were a consequence of inadequate continuous tracking after disbursement.

In several instances, the gaps surfaced only during limit renewals, internal audits, or stress and recovery scenarios—by which time the institution's risk position had already weakened.

## Why this happens

Across many financial institutions, collateral management remains checklist-driven at disbursement, fragmented across legal, credit, and operations, and largely static once the facility is live.

In most cases, there is no single system of record to track document completeness, validity of insurance and legal reports, or key compliance milestones such as ROC filings and charge creation. The result is a risk layer that remains invisible until it becomes urgent.

## What leading banks are doing differently

Forward-looking institutions are moving toward a collateral lifecycle model that embeds visibility and control throughout the life of the exposure.

- Collateral is digitally mapped to each facility.
- Document validity and compliance obligations are continuously monitored.
- Automated alerts are triggered for expiry of insurance and legal reports. Missing documentation and pending compliance actions are surfaced early.
- Risk teams gain portfolio-level visibility, not just deal-level tracking.

This shift enables proactive intervention before documentation gaps impair control, audit readiness, or recovery value.

## Key takeaways

- **Collateral risk is a lifecycle problem:** it does not end at sanction.
- **Manual tracking creates systemic blind spots:** especially as portfolios scale.
- **Continuous monitoring strengthens control:** it supports risk mitigation, audit readiness, and operational efficiency.

## Closing note

As regulatory expectations continue to evolve and portfolios scale in complexity, banks are increasingly exploring technology-led collateral lifecycle management frameworks to ensure continuous assurance, stronger control, and faster issue resolution.
